AI Whispers · an experiment

A report went through six AI handoffs. Most of its facts did not arrive intact.

Teams now chain AI steps together: a report becomes a summary, then a client email, then newsletter bullets, then a sales update. Nobody compares the last message with the first. We did, fact by fact, on six models from three vendors.

6 · The fix

One sentence per step recovers most of it.

We reran the chain two other ways. First, with one extra line in every step: "Keep every figure, caveat and condition from the text exactly as stated, and do not add anything that is not in it." Second, skipping the chain: writing the final 100-word client update straight from the original note.

7 · What to do with this

Three rules for any AI pipeline that passes information along

1. Lock the fragile linesCaveats, conditions and "not yet" statements should travel as fixed text that no step may rewrite. Numbers mostly look after themselves; the sentences that limit them do not.
2. Keep the source in reachLet every step see the original document, not only the previous step's output. Writing from the original kept about twice as many facts as writing from the fifth copy.
3. Tell each step what must surviveA single instruction to preserve figures, caveats and conditions did more than switching to a bigger model. Then test it: run your own chain and compare the end with the start.
This page is that test. Give it one of your real report chains and it shows which facts break, at which step, and which model breaks them least.
How we did it

Method and limits

Method

  • Three invented documents (an analyst note, a drug trial summary, a customer survey), each with 9 to 11 checkable facts. The companies, drug and survey are fictional, so no model could know them.
  • Six handoffs: manager summary, client email, newsletter bullets, news paragraph, morning briefing, client update. Each step sees only the previous step's text.
  • Six models: GPT-4.1 mini, GPT-5.1, Claude Haiku 4.5, Claude Sonnet 4.5, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Pro. One model runs a whole chain.
  • Scoring: after every step, GPT-4.1 compared the text with the original and marked each fact intact, distorted or missing. A plain text search for key figures was run as an independent check.

Limits

  • Small sample: three runs per document and model. The overall pattern is clear; the exact model ranking could shift with more runs.
  • The judge is itself an AI model from one of the tested vendors. "Distorted" includes facts that were only partly kept, such as a figure that lost its comparison.
  • Several steps ask for a shorter text, so some loss is expected. The direct rewrite to the same final length is the fair comparison, and it kept far more.
  • The chain is one plausible pipeline, not every pipeline. Different steps and prompts will lose different things.
  • Models were called through an enterprise gateway with default settings. Newer versions may behave differently.
